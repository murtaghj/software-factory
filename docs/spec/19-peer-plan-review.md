# 19 — Complete engineering plans and focused peer review

## Required behavior

Plans and designs require explicit sign-off by qualified human engineering peers in human mode, or the D5 AI panel in ai_only before the planned implementation begins. This applies to greenfield, existing-project features, bug repairs and the other work types. Fast or one-invocation execution can shorten a proportionate plan, but cannot remove required coverage or independent peer approval. Authorized read-only discovery and bounded baseline inspection may precede the plan; substantive implementation, bootstrap writes and experiments with external effects must fit an approved scope.

Product intent confirmation establishes what the human wants. Engineering peer sign-off establishes that the proposed design, execution, tests and definition of done adequately address that intent. Implementation verification establishes what actually works; release authorization permits a specific delivery action. These decisions have separate subjects and authority. Plan approval does not prove tests pass or authorize production delivery.

The system maintains one complete, versioned PlanReviewPacket. Its summary, structured review and full specification are views of that same packet, with stable links to individual requirements, design decisions and scenarios. The short view must never become a second, drifting plan.

## Contents of a complete plan

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| intent_scope | Problem, user outcomes, accepted intent/requirements, scope, exclusions and measurable acceptance criteria with stable IDs. |
| current_state | Discovery ContextManifest (sources consulted, prior-incident matches, remaining gaps and assumptions); existing behavior, architecture, dependencies, source/contract versions and evidence; greenfield explicitly records nonexistent prior behavior. |
| design | Proposed architecture, component/data flows, state transitions, important alternatives, chosen trade-offs and constraints. Use a small diagram when it clarifies a relationship. |
| interfaces_data | API/events/schema changes, ownership, compatibility, concurrency/idempotency, errors and migration effects; include example payloads where useful. |
| implementation | Ordered work packages, repository/subpath impact, isolated worktree/base strategy, integration dependencies, owners, estimates and resource/budget bounds. |
| acceptance_tests | Criterion-to-scenario matrix; concrete Given/When/Then expectations, positive/negative/boundary/recovery cases, test levels, environment/fixtures and oracle/baseline strategy. |
| quality_security | Applicable performance/reliability/accessibility constraints, authorization/data protection, misuse cases and verification thresholds. |
| delivery_recovery | Explicit report/patch/PR/preview/production scope, build/deployment recipes, rollout ordering, migration rehearsal, rollback/roll-forward and failure handling. |
| operations | Observability, diagnostic evidence, ownership, alerts/runbooks, support and temporary-resource cleanup relevant to the requested outcome. |
| risks_decisions | Risks and impact, mitigations, assumptions, uncertainty, unresolved decisions and accountable owners. Blocking unknowns stay blocking. |
| definition_of_done | Explicit required completion predicates and evidence, including application outcome, verification, reviews, documentation, scoped delivery and cleanup ownership. |
| review_ownership | Authors, independent primary peer, required specialist/service owners, review scope, source provenance and exact approval subject. |

All sections must be present. A section may be not_applicable only with a specific rationale that passes policy and peer review. A documentation fix can use a small plan with concise applicability statements; it does not need a fictional infrastructure design. Missing, empty or generic “TBD” sections are not complete. Risk-selected requirements cannot be suppressed with a not_applicable label.

Every acceptance criterion maps to one or more planned scenarios. Every in-scope behavior has criteria and every definition-of-done item names the evidence needed to establish it. Applicable failure/negative cases are required. A planned scenario is labeled planned; a test recipe or successful plan approval must never render as a passing test result.

Scenario design precedes peer approval; executable test-package authoring may follow it. Later oracle review and canonical package locking retain their own authority. For bugs, the approved plan describes intended baseline red, package review/locking, candidate green and broader regressions. For existing features it covers added and preserved behavior. For greenfield it separately covers foundation readiness and accepted product journeys. Changing a required scenario, oracle or threshold may require plan reapproval and the work-specific baseline to run again.

Definition of done scales to the selected delivery scope. A PR task requires the reviewed, verified candidate and PR/provenance; it does not require an unrequested production deploy. A production task requires the authorized observed release and recovery evidence. A design-scope task (chapter 05) still requires every section: its acceptance_tests and definition_of_done describe the future build, plannedBuildDeliveryScope names that build's scope, and the design's own completion is its delivered approved packet. Unmet mandatory predicates mean incomplete even if source exists or all implementation checkboxes are checked.

The new profile adds ordered implementationTasks with criterion-linked checks and reuseJustifications for new components/dependencies. Non-executable tasks use explicit human/controller evidence. Dependencies require supply-chain review before packet approval; missing work cannot be covered by a deferred-work declaration. See [chapter 21](21-rules-role-content-skills.md) for the authoritative requirements and compatibility rules.

## A layered reading experience

| Layer | What the engineer sees | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision summary | Requested decision, outcome, proposed approach, affected services, delivery scope, consequential risks/blockers and what changed since their last review. | Aim for orientation in about 30 seconds; this is a usability target, not an approval deadline. |
| Structured review | Small design diagram, key choices/trade-offs, acceptance/test matrix, definition of done, rollout/recovery and assigned review scope. | Aim to locate the important reasoning within a few minutes; complex reviews take the time they need. |
| Full specification | Complete sections, contracts, schemas, detailed scenarios, work breakdown, evidence, source links and revision history. | Inspect exact details and resolve questions without losing the review subject. |

Use concise sentences, specific labels, tables for mappings and before/after diagrams when useful. Prefer one idea per review card. Do not turn the full plan into a scrolling wall of generated prose. Do not hide critical/high risks, unresolved blockers, scope expansion or destructive/irreversible effects in collapsed details. If these exceed a short summary, keep them visible even at the cost of a longer initial view.

The review header stays anchored: task, packet revision, requested action, assigned scope and current approval/blocker state. Sections open in place or in a side pane with a stable return location. Deep links target section/criterion/scenario IDs. Keyboard navigation, search, readable focus and save/resume are required. A reviewer can open the full packet or export it at any time.

For a revised plan, show the changed sections, the semantic before/after, affected criteria/tests/DoD and which approvals are invalidated. The full current context remains one action away. Never show “no changes” based only on an unchanged summary; compare the complete referenced artifacts and source pins. Background updates must not replace the revision being reviewed without a visible stale-state notice.

Time on page, scrolling to the bottom, number of expanded sections and “I read this” checkboxes are not proof of review quality. Do not gamify approval speed or pressure engineers to approve within a timer. Test whether reviewers find consequential omissions and can explain what they approved; measure orientation time alongside comprehension and missed risks.

## Peer roles and sign-off

The default is one independent primary engineering peer covering the complete packet, plus required specialists/service owners selected by risk and ownership. A cross-service contract or persistent-data migration normally adds the affected API/data owner; security-sensitive behavior adds the configured security reviewer. Qualified identities and scopes come from trusted organization/application policy. An agent cannot nominate an unqualified approver to bypass a gate.

Authors and substantive human design contributors cannot count as independent approving peers for that revision. In human mode AI review is advisory and cannot supply human sign-off. Under D5 it supplies distinct AI panel decisions; it never supplies a human sign-off field. The intent owner may also act as a peer only if qualified and independent of plan authorship. If no eligible peer is available, record the wait and required assignment; do not auto-approve after a timeout.

In human mode, PlanReviewAssignment records the required peers, their identities, assigned sections and primary/specialist roles. The primary covers every section; specialists review their assigned scope with full context available. The required set is explicit before requesting decisions. Each peer gets a separate approve_plan DecisionRequest bound to requiredPrincipalId and the same PlanReviewSubject. Approval requires confirmation of assigned review scope, no unresolved blocking findings and the current subject. The existing DecisionResponse supports reviewedSectionIds and anchored reviewNotes. Comments and request_changes are retained with author/time/revision; they do not silently mutate the plan.

All required decisions must approve the exact packet/assignment/policy subject. In human mode the coordinator creates an immutable PlanApprovalSet referencing those decisions. The trusted evaluator checks actual authority, independence, scope coverage and unresolved findings rather than trusting an agent-generated approval list. A primary's approval cannot stand in for a missing specialist. Urgent hotfixes require one on-call generalist peer plus every required specialist peer, routed with urgent response targets; INV-21 has no urgent-path carve-out. A peer's product/design approval is not a release authorization.

The UI offers Approve this revision and Request changes, with the subject and scope visible. Do not preselect approval. Responses against stale subjects fail visibly, preserving draft comments. An unchanged retry reuses the original applied decision. In v1, a material revision creates a new packet/subject and requires all assigned peers to reconfirm that subject; the primary reviews the full change and specialists focus on their assigned scope and affected dependencies. Previous decisions remain historical. Automatic carry-forward of specialist approvals across different packet hashes is not part of v1. The UI narrows the reading work with a focused semantic diff while preserving full context. Advisory comments that are resolved without changing the approved subject do not force duplicate approval.

When a parent plan contains child packages, every child must point to a reviewed scope slice and the exact shared constraints/tests/DoD. A child can use parent peer approval only when its complete design and scope were actually covered and the required owners signed that packet. New implementation detail that materially changes the approved design requires a child or revised parent review; a generic parent “go ahead” is insufficient.

## AI panel assignments

The assignment compiler MUST produce exactly one primary covering all twelve PlanSectionId categories, including reasoned not_applicable sections, for architecture and plan gates. Every vote records reviewedSectionIds covering that slot’s full assignment. The trusted risk/ownership policy supplies required specialist scopes independently of the proposed panel; missing roles or sections cannot be removed from the requirements to pass validation. The review coordinator checks these bindings before it aggregates approvals.

For ai_only, the controller converts every primary and specialist/owner slot required by the same trusted assignment policy into an explicit AI role slot; chapter 21 content and qualification apply. It preserves section coverage, complete packet/criteria/DoD, exact subject/assignment bindings and all reapproval rules. The base panel and additional adversarial reviewer required by chapter 05 cannot be replaced by one specialist. Each slot has a distinct qualified configuration and fresh context, and every member must approve. The controller aggregates AIReviewDecision refs only into AIReviewApprovalSet; PlanApprovalSet and requiredPrincipalId human decisions remain human-only. ExecutionPlan, InputBinding and review state use aiReviewApprovalSetRef instead of planApprovalSetRef for this mode.

AI blocking findings use the chapter 09 adjudication path with a distinct qualified AI adjudicator; unresolved disagreement escalates to a human after bounded rounds. Human-authored blocking comments still require their authorized human resolution. Missing specialists, qualifications, current decisions or model-family diversity block, without falling back to a smaller panel. Hotfixes and embargoed work retain human primary and all human specialist approvals.

The review coordinator MUST reconcile findingRefs from every retained vote/round with the immutable finding ledger and its dispositions; a caller-supplied empty openBlockingFindings list cannot clear a finding. Each rejected finding requires an AIReviewAdjudication bound to its task, original subject/assignment/round and decision, with rebuttal and evidence refs, an authorized qualified adjudicator distinct from the authenticated author and every panel configuration, and outcome=rejected. ai-review/1.0 permits one rebuttal and one adjudication per gate review episode; changing the subject, retrying or continuing does not reset that bound. An upheld finding, missing disposition or exceeded bound blocks and escalates to human. A fixed finding instead requires independent verification evidence on a changed subject. After either resolution, every panel member must submit a fresh vote in a later round on the current subject, after the disposition time. The coordinator persists prior decisions, findings and dispositions across rework; tools/review_mode_validation.py models these cross-artifact predicates without claiming real adjudicator effectiveness.

Every finding-bearing vote MUST strictly precede its disposition, which MUST strictly precede every fresh current-round vote. Historical and current votes must fall within authority and at or before evaluation time; later repository binding cannot retroactively authorize a PR vote. Qualification is resolved again for every referenced reviewer configuration at use time, including historical votes.

## Contracts and execution

PlanReviewPacket contains task/spec identity, packet revision, work type, target context, author identities, compact summary, the complete section manifest, criteria, planned scenarios, DoD items and structured risks. Section contentRef links immutable full detail. Criterion/scenario/section IDs stay stable across revisions where meaning remains stable. Model-written summaries are checked against the detailed packet and critical-risk inventory before review publication.

PlanReviewSubject binds packetRef, assignmentRef and policyRef. In human mode PlanApprovalSet references the exact subject and required human decisions; ai_only instead uses AIReviewPanelAssignment → AIReviewSubject → AIReviewApprovalSet with separate aiReviewApprovalSetRef bindings; state.approvalSetRef remains human-only. The assignment names packet content and roles; the subject names that assignment, packet, policy and InputBinding refs; decisions name the subject. The InputBinding is the pre-review stage binding and cannot contain this approval. This directed binding avoids circular artifact hashes. ExecutionPlan refers to reviewPacketRef and, after approval, planApprovalSetRef in human mode or aiReviewApprovalSetRef in ai_only; InputBinding carries those refs for execution governed by the plan. The scheduler's ExecutionPlan DAG is not a substitute for the complete engineering plan. The compiler validates that the executable graph matches the signed scope, work packages, gates, scenarios and limits.

The plan_peer_review stage follows completed planning/design and precedes bootstrap or substantive execution/test-package authoring. Its controller may gather automated completeness/adversarial review evidence, then, in human mode, persist human decision requests and enter waiting_decision without occupying a runner. This controller stage uses timeoutClass human_wait for the configured decision/calendar deadline; short model/persistence Activities keep their own execution timeouts. Completion requires plan_peer_approved and plan_accepted; greenfield additionally requires architecture_accepted. Initial plan authoring does not pass its own approval gate.

GET /tasks/{taskId}/plan-review exposes the current PlanReviewView with packet/subject/assignment refs, phase, required decisions and blockers. Complete content uses authorized artifact access. Only the existing decision response endpoint receives human sign-off or changes requested; respond_decision rejects confirm_intent, approve_plan and approve_rule_exception with 422 decision_requires_response_endpoint. For approve_plan, the server validates reviewedSectionIds against the caller's assignment, persists anchored notes with the immutable decision and rejects self/service/stale/incomplete approvals. Additional discussion before final response uses the review-comment API, with task/subject binding and the common command inbox. Neither comments nor an enabled UI button can set a trusted gate directly. resolve_plan_comment uses the common command endpoint with the original comment ID, current subject and resolutionRef. Only the originating peer or a recorded qualified independent delegate can resolve a blocking comment; preserve the author/time/evidence of that disposition. A new packet revision alone does not resolve an old blocker.

Apply current task/subject revisions again in the serialized workflow handler. Pending comments and material change requests suspend final approval while reconciled; an arriving approval cannot race past a known blocking finding. The authoritative handler, not the dashboard projection, resolves this order. Rework goes back to the author/design/criteria owner with specific findings, then publishes a new packet and focused diff. Changed intent first returns to the intent owner where required; changed design cannot silently redefine accepted intent.

## Build from an approved design

A later implementation task MAY name a delivered design through TaskRequest.designSource (the “Build from this design” action). Its work type, target context and application MUST match the design, and its delivery scope MUST be patch, pr, preview or production. The controller appends a DesignBuildBinding at admission, before the plan node, and again before every later gate or effect that relies on the plan approval.

The task MAY reuse the packet and skip re-authoring ONLY IF all of these hold: the binding names the exact delivered packet and PlanReviewSubject hash; the current DesignBasis equals the recorded one (the tree of every pinned source path at the build base, the application snapshot, the resolved rules bundle, the policy, the accepted intent/TaskSpec version, the digest of the build's own accepted intent and acceptance criteria, the build's resolved assurance tier and required-specialist assignment, and a build delivery scope equal to plannedBuildDeliveryScope). The controller derives these current values from the build task's accepted spec and policy, never from the binding's own assertion; every approval is before approvalsExpireAt; and for ai_only approvals the application's ReviewModeDecision is in force at the binding's evaluatedAt (not revoked, expired, emergency-stopped, degraded or superseded by a new qualification cohort) AND the build task itself is eligible under it: the decision's application, work type, change class and assurance scope cover the build, and the build is not security-flagged, embargoed, incident-priority or a hotfix. Otherwise this is review_mode drift and every recorded approval gate is requested again under human requirements. The controller evaluates this from the decision and the build's control-plane snapshot, never from a caller flag; storage rejects an ai_only reuse row whose decision is not in force for the builder. On reuse, the plan node adopts the packet through a controller step with no authoring invocation, and the plan review controller evaluates its gates against the design's existing approval sets on that identical subject. InputBinding and ExecutionPlan carry reviewPacketRef, the design approval-set refs and designBuildBindingRef. The build still runs its own oracle, implementation, verification, review and delivery gates; a reused design never satisfies them.

Any drift records result replan, its drift kinds, the reopened sections and the re-requested gates, then follows the existing revision path above: the planner revises the reopened sections in a new packet revision linked by previousPacketRef, publishes the focused semantic diff, and the changed subject goes back to review. Only the affected gates are re-requested: architecture_accepted, plan_peer_approved and plan_accepted when any section reopens or rules/policy changed; intent_aligned only when greenfield intent changed, returning first to the intent owner; and every recorded approval gate when approvals expired or the AI authority that supplied them lapsed. Within a re-requested gate the v1 rule still applies: every assigned peer reconfirms the new subject using the focused diff, and no specialist approval carries forward across packet hashes.

| Drift | Sections reopened |
| --- | --- |
| source_pins | current_state, design, interfaces_data, implementation, acceptance_tests, risks_decisions |
| application_snapshot | current_state, design, interfaces_data, risks_decisions |
| rules | implementation, acceptance_tests, quality_security, review_ownership |
| policy | quality_security, delivery_recovery, review_ownership |
| intent | intent_scope, design, acceptance_tests, definition_of_done |
| assurance | acceptance_tests, quality_security, review_ownership |
| build_scope | delivery_recovery, definition_of_done |
| approvals_expired, review_mode | None; the same subject is re-reviewed |

Approvals never transfer silently across a changed subject (INV-22). A binding that names another packet or subject, reuses despite drift, or omits an affected section or gate is rejected. If drift appears after reuse, such as a moving base that changes a pinned path, the same replan path runs before further dependent work. Only a task in the same security group may bind an embargoed design; that build inherits the design's embargo and group, like a child task (chapter 02), and other-group or ungrouped builders are rejected. tools/design_delivery_validation.py models these predicates on static fixtures; real currency resolution is **not yet proven**.

## Storage, build ownership and compatibility

Use immutable object artifacts for packet sections, scenarios, assignments, subjects, approvals and review reports; Postgres stores current task review refs/revision and durable comments/commands/decisions; Temporal owns waits and progression. Comments retain section/criterion/scenario anchors and original author/source binding. Continue-As-New preserves the plan reference and pending decision/comment identities via the existing command/decision state, with planReviewStateRef where needed. No native harness session is required to reconstruct a review.

W-072 implements packets, completeness checks, peer assignment/sign-off and execution binding. W-073 implements the layered reading experience and anchored discussion/diff. W-074 qualifies coverage, independent authority, stale-subject/comment races, material rework and comprehension. AC-85–AC-94 are required for full v1, with core peer review available before the M3 implementation pilot. W-144 adds design-only delivery and build-from-design binding (AC-304–AC-318).

Specification revision 1.3 introduces this requirement. That revision used greenfield 3.0.0 and existing_feature/bug_fix 2.0.0. Revision 1.4 advances them to 4.0.0 and 3.0.0 respectively while preserving these human gates. Other work-type templates must include the same mandatory peer-review stage and pass conformance before release. Domain envelopes remain 2.0; the API description advances to 2.2.0 on /v2. Deploy compatible validators/workers before enabling these templates; retain or explicitly migrate old histories. Existing approvals do not silently acquire a wider meaning.

## Structure outline and proportional review

PlanReviewPacket.structureOutline is required by the 1.4 qualification profile and new journey templates. It contains changed component/path boundaries, proposed function/type/interface signatures, API/event/data changes, vertical slices with criterion IDs, and explicit exclusions. Existing projects include the current-to-proposed boundary; greenfield includes the first foundation/product slices. Use short concrete entries and linked contract definitions instead of reproducing entire files. No applicable change is hidden because it lacks a conventional function signature.

Place the outline immediately after the requested outcome/decision and before extended prose. The full packet remains canonical: summary, outline, scenarios, risks and DoD share the exact same approval subject. A structure outline assists review but cannot replace concurrency, security, rollout or failure analysis. Review scope and detail scale with risk; all 12 categories retain applicable coverage and independent approval under ReviewMode before implementation. D5 replaces human review with a qualified panel for opted-in applications; omission of pre-implementation approval and preapproved tiny-change exemptions remain excluded.

In human mode, pilot bug/feature oracle review is also human; that review may be batched into the same review session once executable checks exist, but its package binding and the engineering-plan approval remain separate decisions. In human mode, ordinary human PR approval precedes merge and is tracked through the explicit completion policy. The layer design reduces navigation/reading overhead without inferring approval from attention telemetry or weakening exact-subject authority.

Campaign coverage follows chapter 23: one independent primary peer covers the full packet and each target's confirmed owners are required specialist peers for that target. Missing owner approval blocks that target; retargeting creates a new subject and every assigned peer reconfirms. No approval carries forward automatically.
